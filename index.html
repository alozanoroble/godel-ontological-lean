Modal logic · 1970 – 2002

Gödel's God

Kurt Gödel left a proof of God's existence in his notes: five axioms, three definitions, three theorems. It is also self-contradictory — and nobody noticed for forty-three years. Here is every line of it, what went wrong, and the four repairs.

Three words you need first

□ p   ◇ p

Possible worlds

To make “must” and “could” precise, imagine a catalogue of complete ways reality might have gone. Ours is one entry. □p means p holds in every entry; ◇p means in at least one.

φ, ψ

Properties

Anything a thing can be — tall, kind, prime. The argument quantifies over all properties, and over properties of properties, so keep the two levels apart.

P(φ)

Positive

The one undefined word. Roughly: good in itself, a perfection. Gödel never says what it means — he only lays down rules it obeys. The argument is whatever “positive” means, if it behaves like this, then…
